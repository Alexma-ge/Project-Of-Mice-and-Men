# Project Of Mice and Men

### *"Seeing what was read: How non-visual prior knowledge shapes neural synchrony during naturalistic movie watching"*


Prior knowledge shapes how sensory information is processed and interpreted, yet the extent to which prior knowledge and expectations influence neural processing remains incompletely understood. The present study investigated whether non-visual prior knowledge acquired through reading influences neural synchrony during naturalistic movie watching. Participants who had read John Steinbeck’s “Of Mice and Men” (1937) were compared with participants who had no prior exposure to the narrative while both groups watched the movie adaptation of the novel during functional magnetic resonance imaging. Inter-subject correlation analyses were used to examine whether neural responses were more strongly synchronized within the Familiar or Unfamiliar group. The Unfamiliar group showed stronger synchrony mainly in posterior visual regions, suggesting stronger alignment with the perceptual structure of the movie. In contrast, the Familiar group showed stronger synchrony in smaller and more distributed temporal, frontal and occipital regions, suggesting that non-visual prior knowledge may have shifted processing toward higher-level interpretation, memory-based processing and narrative integration. The ISC clusters showed only minimal overlap with localizer-defined face- and scene-selective regions, indicating that the observed group differences were not primarily restricted to classical category-selective visual areas. Overall, the findings suggest that non-visual prior knowledge can modulate neural synchrony during naturalistic viewing.
